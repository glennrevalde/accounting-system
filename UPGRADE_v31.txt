G-SMaRT Accounting - version 31 (2026-11-26)
=============================================

This package holds only the files that were added or changed since version 30.
Copy the accounting-system folder over your installation, keeping the folder
structure. config/config.php is not included and is never changed.

Database
--------
Run database/migrations/2026_11_26_average_cost_books.sql once on every company
database (phpMyAdmin > Import, Navicat > Execute SQL File, or the mysql command).
It is written for MySQL 5.5, adds no table or column, changes no posted amount and is
safe to run twice. Until it is run, the program stops after log-in and names it.
It does three things:
 1. sets the quantities on hand by site (item_site_stock) to the totals of the
    inventory movements;
 2. sets every average cost to the remaining value divided by the remaining quantity;
 3. adds the report Inventory > Reports > Average Cost Changes, for the roles that may
    open Inventory Valuation Detail.

Then, once, as an administrator:
 - press Ctrl+F5 in the browser (new assets/css/app.css);
 - System Administration > Utilities: Verify & Rebuild Data > Rebuild data (costs).
   The processing orders and sales that were costed at the overstated averages
   (for example PRC-00355 and PRC-00356 at 462.69) are costed again in date order,
   and so are the cuts they produced. Then click Verify Data: "Quantities on hand
   match the movements" and "Average cost matches the value on hand" must be OK.

What was wrong (the requests of "ADDITIONAL_REVISED FEATURES ... v31.xlsx")
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Item 607 Pork Carcass: bills at 225.00 (BILL-00204, -00210, -00211, -00212) showed
averages after of 625.64 and 462.69, the processing orders PRC-00355 and PRC-00356
took the carcass out at 462.69, and the Inventory Center said "The quantity on hand
(-4.81) differs from the total of its movements (91.86) by -96.67."

 - Cause: the average was the value of the movements divided by the quantity of the
   quick-lookup table item_site_stock, and that table had 96.67 kg less than the
   movements. That happened like this: a processing order that uses all of a bill
   leaves the site at zero; Rebuild Data removed the site rows that were at zero; when
   the processing order (or the bill) was saved again later, its old movement was
   reversed with an UPDATE of a row that was no longer there, so nothing was taken
   off, and the new movement then added the row again with -96.67. From then on every
   receipt divided the right value by a quantity 96.67 too small. In a test copy, a
   bill of 96.67, a processing order of 96.67, Rebuild Data, the order saved again and
   then bills of 91.86, 59.10 and 37.22 at 225.00 give exactly -4.81 against 91.86,
   then averages of 625.64 and 462.69, the figures of the screenshot. With version 31
   the same steps give 225.00 every time.
 - It is fixed in three places: the average cost is now always worked out on the
   quantity of the movements (the "accumulated remaining value divided by the
   existing quantity balance"); the table is always written with an insert-or-update,
   so a removed row is added back; and an item whose table still differs is put right
   the next time it moves (the migration puts all of them right at once).

How processing orders (Disassemble / Process Items) move the average cost
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Processing orders were where the jumps showed, for these reasons:
 1. They used whole bills, which left sites at zero, the case that let the
    quantities table drift (above, fixed).
 2. Their inputs go out at the average of all sites, so a wrong input average passes
    into every output (the cuts of PRC-00355/356 inherited 462.69). Rebuild data
    costs them again.
 3. Outputs received while they were below zero (cuts sold at the counter before the
    processing order was entered): the whole difference between the old average and
    the new cost stayed on the few units left. Example: 9.9 kg sold below zero at
    225.00, then 10 kg received at 300.00 gave an average of 7,725.00 on the 0.1 kg
    left. Now the receipt settles the shortfall at its own cost and posts the
    difference to cost of goods sold (742.50 in the example): Dr Cost of Goods Sold,
    Cr Inventory, memo "Stock sold below zero, settled at the cost received". The
    average stays 300.00. The same applies to the input when a processing order is
    entered before the day's bill, and to every receipt (bills, item receipts,
    builds, adjustments up, returns). Transfers between sites settle nothing.
 4. Each output takes its share of the cost by the allocation method. With Physical
    units or Weight / measure every kilo costs the same (trimmings as much as chops);
    with Relative sales value or Fixed percentages the dearer cuts take more and the
    unit costs are steadier from order to order; with Current average cost
    (difference to variance) the outputs come in at their own average, which then
    does not move. When yields or shares change from one order to the next, the
    output averages move with them: that is correct costing, not an error.
 5. A saved order is costed at the averages of the moment it is saved, not of its
    date, and saving an old order again issues its inputs at today's average. Rebuild
    data works every cost out again in date order.

What is changed
 1. Costing (app/Stock.php, app/Posting.php): the average on the movements, the
    quantities table kept in step, shortfalls settled at the cost received, and a
    fix of the "Quantity and total value" adjustment, which read a cost it did not
    have when the item had no average yet.
 2. New report Inventory > Reports > Average Cost Changes: every transaction that
    changed an item's average, in the order the costs were worked out, with Qty, Unit
    cost, On hand before, Avg before, Avg after, Change, Change % (25% or more in
    red) and What moved it; filters Change of at least (1% to 100%) and Processing,
    disassembly and builds only. The Inventory Center has an "Average cost changes"
    button for the item.
 3. Inventory Center: Value (all sites) is the remaining value of the movements; a
    history line with no quantity says "Value adjustment" or "Below-zero stock
    settled at this cost"; the reorder figure of an item at or below its reorder
    point is shown in plain orange text instead of a badge.
 4. Utilities: Verify Data also counts a site that has movements but no quantity
    row; Rebuild data ends by checking every quantity against its movements.

The user's manual (Help button, Home > User's Manual) and the Word edition
(GSMaRT_Accounting_Users_Manual_v31.docx) are updated for all of the above.
